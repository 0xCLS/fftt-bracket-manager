Player & Tournament Guide

Forging Fellowship Table Tennis Games 3
Ypsilanti Seventh-day Adventist Church · 402 S. Adams St., Ypsilanti, MI 48197

All skill levels welcome! We are here to enjoy friendly competition, encourage one another, and build community through fellowship. Players, family, friends and spectators are welcome.

All games are win by at least two points. For example, 11–9 wins but 11–10 does not; 21–19 wins but 21–20 does not.

Championship, Consolation and byes

  • Win a Championship match: Advance in Championship.
  • Lose your first actually played Championship match: You may be eligible for Consolation. The Bracket Manager reviews candidates and builds the separate bracket; a second match is not guaranteed.
  • Lose after already winning a played Championship match: Your singles run ends; you do not drop into Consolation.
  • Receive a bye: Advance without playing that round. A bye does not count as a played win or loss. If you then lose your first actual match, you may still be eligible for Consolation.
  • Consolation: Win to advance; lose and your singles run ends. The Consolation Champion does not return to Championship.

Example: Maya loses her first played Championship match and may be considered for Consolation. Jordan receives a bye, then loses the first match Jordan actually plays; Jordan may also be eligible. Eli wins an opening match and later loses, so Eli does not enter Consolation.

This is not traditional double elimination. We hope everyone enjoys plenty of play, but bracket size, attendance and timing may limit second matches.

Scoring, serving and changing ends

  • Know your format: Championship is best of three games to 11. Consolation and Doubles are one game to 21. If uncertain, ask the Scorekeeper before starting.
  • Service rotation in singles: Change server every two points. At 10–10 in an 11-point game or 20–20 in a 21-point game, change server after every point, and continue until someone leads by two.
  • Serving (recommended): Start with the ball on an open palm, toss it upward about 6 inches (16 cm), and strike it as it falls while keeping it visible. It bounces once on your side and then your opponent's side.
  • Who serves first? Use a coin toss or another random choice. The winner may choose to serve first, receive first or choose the table end; the other player makes the remaining choice.
  • Changing ends: Players switch table ends after each game. No additional FFTT3 mid-game change is prescribed in these approved instructions.
  • Respect each other: Call the score, make sure your opponent is ready, help new players learn and ask the Scorekeeper for assistance if unsure.

How Mixed-Skill Doubles works

The two Championship finalists anchor the teams. Each is paired with one selected Consolation player; partners do not have to be Consolation finalists. This is one game to 21 points, win by two, and does not change singles standings.

  1. Serve diagonally. The served ball bounces on the server's right half and then on the receiver's right half. After service, rally returns may land anywhere on the opponents' side.
  2. Take turns hitting. Every rally goes server → receiver → server's partner → receiver's partner, then repeats. Partners take alternating shots.
  3. Rotate servers. The server changes after every two points. At 20–20, the server changes after every point.
  4. Replay a let. If an otherwise legal serve touches the net, replay it without scoring a point.
  5. Win by two. Examples: 21–19, 22–20; 21–20 is not enough.

Serving example: Team A is A + B and Team B is C + D. If A first serves to C, rally hits follow A → C → B → D → A, repeated. Serving/receiving pairings rotate A serves to C → C serves to B → B serves to D → D serves to A. After 20–20, use the same order with one point per server.

The Scorekeeper explains the first server/receiver and confirms the score. The Bracket Manager or Organizer chooses the teams. No additional mid-game end change has been approved for this single game. Do not assume one; any later Organizer-approved change must be communicated consistently to players and staff before play.

Before, during and after your match

Before: Check in under your registered name by the announced cutoff, listen for your assigned table, and confirm your match format with the Scorekeeper.

During: Keep the play area clear, call the score.

After: Confirm the result with the Scorekeeper. If uncertain, ask the Bracket Manager whether you have another match; please stay and cheer for others!
